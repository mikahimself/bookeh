bookeh examples: cover treatment

Two questions in one page. Layout (sections A–C): where the title and author go now that the placeholder carries them itself. Treatment (the bar): the frame round every cover, photos included, and a coloured, noise-textured cloth for the placeholder. The bar applies to every panel, so you can try any layout with any treatment.

The four covers drawn in CSS stand in for photos: a white one and a black one on purpose, because those are the covers that disappear into the ground without a frame. The rest are placeholders. Women is lent; Heavier than heaven is ordered (airmail band). The noise is SVG feTurbulence (Perlin-based fractalNoise), one tiny data-URI tile blended over a per-book colour picked by hashing the title. No images, no canvas, no JS in the real thing.

A. Caption only under photosmy pick

A photo keeps its title and author underneath. A placeholder already wears them, so it has nothing underneath. Each fact appears once. Rows go slightly ragged at the foot, because a placeholder ends at its cover's bottom edge. The grid aligns to the top, so that reads as books of different heights, not as a mistake. The lent marker moves onto the placeholder, before its title. In select mode a placeholder gets a short line under it holding only the checkbox.

B. Covers only

No captions at all; the shelf is a wall of covers. Photos carry their title in the artwork, placeholders in type. The calmest of the three, but you lose the clean text title under photos, which matters for scanning at s and for photos whose type is tiny. The lent marker becomes an 8px square on the cover's top-right corner. The detail panel and rows view stay the place to read.

C. Caption under all, placeholder keeps the title only

Rows stay even: every tile has the same caption. The placeholder drops the author and keeps only the title, as the cloth's "spine label". It still repeats the title. Included as the even-rows baseline, not recommended.

Size s, layout A

At s (70px columns) the placeholder carries the title only, as today. The texture tile scales with the cover, so it stays a texture and does not become blotches.
